The shape of it

Before the vocabulary, the sentence it all serves.

A satellite passes over California twice a day and reports which 375 m cells were on fire. From those reports we reconstruct where each fire's edge was at a sequence of times. Given the edge now, plus the weather, the fuel and the terrain around it, predict where the edge will be 24 hours from now. Everything below is machinery in service of that one sentence.

Satellite fire detection

Where the labels come from — and the source of most of this project's hard constraints.

Active fire detection
A satellite observation that a given ground cell was emitting fire-like heat at the moment of overpass. Critically it is not a burned-area product: it says "burning now", not "burned at some point". A fire that flares and dies between two overpasses is never recorded.
FIRMS Fire Information for Resource Management System
NASA's distribution service for active fire detections. Serves CSV over a REST API keyed by a MAP_KEY. Each row is one detection: latitude, longitude, acquisition time, confidence, FRP.
in this project741,236 detections across 2015–2023 for the California bounding box, 67 MB, the complete label source.
VIIRS Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite
The instrument. Its fire product resolves to 375 m cells — considerably finer than the 1 km MODIS heritage product, which is why it is the right choice for perimeter work.
S-NPP and NOAA-20 the spacecraft
Two satellites carrying VIIRS. S-NPP has flown since 2011; NOAA-20 joined in 2018.
the catchAdding NOAA-20 would double the observations, but only from 2018 — so early training years would be observed half as often as late ones, and the model would learn the sensor's history rather than the fire's. We use S-NPP alone for homogeneity.
Sun-synchronous orbit
An orbit that crosses every latitude at the same local solar time each day. It gives consistent illumination, and it is the reason coverage is not continuous but arrives in fixed daily slots.
the catchThis single fact reshaped the project. All 741,236 detections fall in two ~4-hour bands — 08–11Z and 19–22Z, about 11 hours apart. Nothing is ever seen in the other 16 hours, so CLAUDE.md's 6-hour windows were 59.6% empty and we moved to 24.
Overpass · revisit
An overpass is one transit of the satellite over a location; the revisit interval is the time until the next. For one polar orbiter at these latitudes: roughly twice daily.
Swath · scan and track
The swath is the ground strip the sensor images in one pass. Cells near the swath edge are viewed obliquely and cover more ground than those at nadir — FIRMS reports each detection's actual footprint as scan and track in km.
in this projectNot yet used. We approximate the footprint with a fixed dilation instead, which is why perimeters needed calibrating against known acreage.
FRP Fire Radiative Power
Radiative energy release rate in megawatts — a proxy for how intensely a cell is burning, not merely whether it is. Available as a feature; not yet used.
Confidence low / nominal / high
VIIRS's own categorical quality flag. Low-confidence detections include sun glint, hot bare soil and industrial heat.
in this projectFiltered to nominal and above, per CLAUDE.md.
Detection type
A classification field: 0 vegetation fire, 1 volcano, 2 other static land source, 3 offshore.
in this projectFiltered to type 0. Without it, gas flares and industrial hot spots persist across years and register as permanent phantom fires.
NRT vs SP near-real-time vs standard processing
NRT is published within hours using predicted orbit data and covers only the recent past. SP is the reprocessed archive with final geolocation.
in this projectVIIRS_SNPP_SP — NRT reaches back roughly two months and cannot serve a 2015–2023 study.

Weather

Wind is the dominant driver of short-term spread; humidity and temperature govern how readily fuel ignites.

HRRR High-Resolution Rapid Refresh
NOAA's 3 km hourly weather model for the continental US, updated every hour. Our source for wind, temperature and humidity. Archived publicly on S3 back to mid-2014.
Analysis vs forecast hour
The analysis (f00, marked anl) is the model's best estimate of conditions now, having assimilated observations. f01, f02… are projections forward. For reconstructing past weather you want analysis at every hour, not a single run's forecast chain.
GRIB2
The binary format meteorological data ships in. A file is a concatenation of independent messages, each one variable at one level at one time — which is what makes partial retrieval possible at all.
.idx sidecar
A plain-text index published beside each GRIB2 file giving the byte offset of every message. Read the index, compute the byte ranges you need, request only those.
in this projectEach HRRR surface file is ~109 MB; the four fields we need are 4.7 MB. Byte-range subsetting is a 23x saving, and merging adjacent messages into contiguous runs cuts the request count further.
UGRD · VGRD
Wind decomposed into components: UGRD eastward, VGRD northward, both at 10 m above ground. Components rather than speed-and-bearing because direction is circular — 359° and 1° are adjacent in reality but maximally distant numerically, which wrecks both interpolation and gradients.
RH · TMP
Relative humidity and temperature at 2 m. Together they set how dry the fine fuels are.
in this projectAt the Camp Fire ignition cell we measured wind from 073° at 22 mph with RH 10% — the Jarbo Gap offshore event, recovered correctly from the subset.
Reanalysis and ERA5
A retrospective, physically consistent reconstruction of past weather. ERA5 is the global one at ~31 km — far coarser than HRRR but reaching back to 1940, the fallback for pre-2014 fires.

Fuels & terrain

What is available to burn, and the shape of the ground it sits on. Both change slowly, so both are treated as static per fire.

LANDFIRE
A US interagency programme mapping vegetation, fuel and disturbance at 30 m, republished periodically as versioned snapshots (LF2016, LF2022, LF2023…).
Fuel model · FBFM40 Scott & Burgan 40
A categorical scheme assigning each cell one of 40 standard fuel types — grass, shrub, timber litter, slash — each with laboratory-characterised burning behaviour. Categorical, so it resamples by nearest neighbour or majority, never by averaging: the mean of "grass" and "timber" is meaningless.
CC · CH canopy cover, canopy height
Fraction of ground shaded by tree crowns, and their height. Together they govern whether fire stays on the surface or climbs into the crowns, and how much the canopy shelters surface wind.
LFPS LANDFIRE Product Service
The REST API that clips and reprojects LANDFIRE layers on demand: submit a job with a layer list and an area, poll, download a GeoTIFF.
the catchOnly LF2016, LF2022 and LF2023 carry fuels — LF2014 and LF2020 ship disturbance and topography only. And since LANDFIRE encodes burn scars, using a version published after a fire leaks that fire's own footprint into its input.
DEM digital elevation model
A raster of ground elevation. Slope and aspect are derived from it rather than stored.
SRTM · 3DEP
Two elevation sources: SRTM is the 30 m near-global shuttle radar product; 3DEP is the USGS programme, 10 m over the US and largely lidar-derived.
COG cloud-optimised GeoTIFF
A GeoTIFF internally tiled and indexed so a reader can fetch just the region it needs over HTTP without downloading the file.
in this project3DEP tiles are 468 MB each; a windowed read of the Camp Fire footprint pulls 56 MB. The same idea as the HRRR .idx trick, in a different format.
Slope · aspect
Steepness, and the compass direction a hillside faces. Fire runs uphill far faster than down — flames preheat the fuel above them. Aspect governs sun exposure and therefore how dry that fuel is.
in this projectAspect is stored as sin and cos rather than degrees, for the same circular-discontinuity reason as wind.
TPI topographic position index
A cell's elevation minus the mean of its neighbourhood. Positive on ridges, negative in valleys, near zero on uniform slopes — a compact encoding of landform that raw elevation does not provide.
Dead fuel moisture · the Fosberg equations
Water content of dead fuel as a percentage of dry weight — the single strongest control on whether fire spreads at all. 10-hour fuel refers to sticks roughly 6–25 mm thick, which equilibrate with the air over about ten hours. Fosberg's equations estimate it from temperature and humidity.

Geospatial machinery

Four sources on four different grids. Most pipeline bugs in this domain are quiet misalignments, not crashes.

CRS coordinate reference system
The rule mapping numbers to places on Earth. Two layers can only be combined once they are in the same CRS.
EPSG:4326 WGS84 lat/lon
Degrees of latitude and longitude — the GPS standard, and how FIRMS reports. Unsuitable for modelling: a degree of longitude is ~111 km at the equator and ~82 km in northern California, so square pixels are not square ground.
EPSG:5070 Albers Equal Area CONUS
A projection in metres, tuned for the continental US, that preserves area. Equal-area matters here because burned area is the quantity being predicted and scored.
in this projectThe modelling CRS. Everything is resampled to a 100 m EPSG:5070 grid — clustering, rasterisation and tiling all agree because they share it.
Lambert Conformal Conic
HRRR's native projection. Conformal preserves angles rather than areas — sensible for wind, wrong for burned area, hence reprojection into 5070.
Raster · vector
A raster is a grid of cells; a vector is points, lines and polygons. FIRMS arrives as vector points; the model consumes raster.
Rasterisation
Converting vector geometry into a grid, deciding which cells a shape occupies.
Affine transform
Six numbers mapping a raster's row and column indices to real-world coordinates: pixel size, rotation, and the origin corner. Storing it alongside an array is what makes the array geographic rather than merely rectangular.
Resampling nearest / bilinear / majority
Moving values onto a new grid. Nearest copies the closest cell, correct for categories. Bilinear interpolates, correct for continuous fields like elevation. Majority takes the commonest value in the source footprint, correct for downsampling categories.
the catchBilinear on FBFM40 would average fuel-model codes and invent classes that do not exist.
Windowed read · byte-range request
Fetching only the needed bytes of a remote file instead of the whole thing. The single most valuable technique in this pipeline — it is what keeps 204 GB of HRRR and 25 GB of elevation off the disk.

Fire behaviour

The physical vocabulary the model is implicitly learning, and which the failure modes are described in.

Perimeter · burn scar
The perimeter is the boundary of the burned region at a moment; the burn scar is the lasting footprint left behind, visible in imagery and encoded into later fuel maps.
Head · flank · backing
The head runs with the wind and moves fastest; the flanks spread sideways; the backing edge creeps against the wind, sometimes orders of magnitude slower. One fire therefore has several very different spread rates at once, which is exactly why a purely radial model is a weak baseline.
in this projectVisible in the Camp Fire figure: the westward run in the first day, then slow eastward backing over the following week.
Run
A period of rapid wind- or slope-driven advance. Most of a large fire's total area is typically accumulated in a handful of runs lasting hours.
Spotting ember transport
Embers lofted ahead of the front igniting new fire beyond it — sometimes kilometres out. Appears in the data as burning that is disconnected from the perimeter, and will read as a false negative even when the model is behaving sensibly.
Offshore · downslope wind
Wind reversing the usual onshore pattern, descending terrain and compressing as it falls, which warms and dries it. California's are named — Diablo in the north, Santa Ana in the south. They drive the most destructive fires.
in this projectThe Camp Fire's 073°/22 mph/10% RH is a textbook instance, and it validated the HRRR pipeline end to end.
Suppression
Firefighting. It bends spread in ways no public dataset records, so a suppressed fire and a free-burning fire look identical in the inputs while behaving differently — an irreducible noise floor.
FARSITE · Rothermel
Rothermel's 1972 equations are the physics of surface fire spread; FARSITE is the operational simulator built on them. FARSITE is the benchmark to beat — and it needs hand-specified conditions per run, which is where a learned model can win on speed.

The learning problem

How a continuous physical process becomes a finite set of supervised examples.

Timestep · window
The discretisation of time. A window is the interval whose detections form one snapshot; a timestep is one such snapshot in a fire's sequence.
in this project24 hours, forced by the twice-daily revisit. 7,709 windows across 692 fires, 8.9% of them empty.
Label vs target
The label is what is known at prediction time — cumulative burned area up to T, an input. The target is what must be predicted — newly burned area from T to T+24h. Confusing the two is the classic way to build a model that scores brilliantly and knows nothing.
Horizon
How far ahead the prediction reaches. Must match the window here, since a target cannot be finer than the observations composing it.
Patch · tile · stride
A patch is the fixed-size crop the network consumes (256×256 at 100 m = 25.6 km square). Tiling covers a large fire with several patches; the stride is the step between their origins, so a 128 px stride on a 256 px tile means 50% overlap.
in this projectTiling was chosen because a single centred patch retained only 58.5% of windows for the largest fires — megafires would have been represented only while small.
Class imbalance · pos_weight
Burning cells are vastly outnumbered by unburned ones, so a model predicting "nothing burns" scores 99.99% accuracy. pos_weight scales the loss on positive pixels to compensate.
in this projectMedian positive fraction 0.011%, implying pos_weight around 9,400 — and one of the arguments against a larger patch, which would have made it 3.5x worse.
Leakage
Information reaching the model that would not exist at genuine prediction time. It inflates scores and is often invisible until deployment.
the catchThree distinct routes here: tiles of one fire split across train and test; LANDFIRE versions postdating a fire and encoding its own scar; and any future weather leaking into the input window.
Fire-level split
Partitioning train/validation/test by whole fire, never by sample. Every timestep and tile of a fire stays on one side of the line.
Augmentation
Synthetic training variety through transformation. Constrained here: flips must also negate the corresponding wind component, arbitrary rotation is invalid because terrain and prevailing wind are not rotationally symmetric, and intensity jitter is meaningless on a binary mask.
Normalisation
Rescaling each channel to comparable range so no input dominates by unit alone. Statistics must be computed on the training split only — deriving them from all data leaks test information.

Model architecture

Two ideas combined: something that reasons over space, and something that reasons over time.

U-Net
An encoder–decoder network for dense prediction. The encoder downsamples to capture context, the decoder upsamples back to full resolution, and the output is one value per input pixel.
Skip connection
A direct link from an encoder layer to the matching decoder layer, restoring the fine spatial detail that downsampling discards. Without them a U-Net produces blurred, spatially imprecise boundaries.
ConvLSTM
An LSTM whose internal operations are convolutions rather than matrix multiplications, so its memory is a spatial map rather than a vector. It can carry "the front has been advancing north-east for two days" as a structured, located memory.
Probability map
The output: a per-pixel burn probability in [0,1] via sigmoid, thresholded to a binary mask for scoring. Keeping probabilities allows calibration assessment and a tunable operational threshold.
Weighted BCE · focal loss · Dice
Three responses to imbalance. Weighted BCE multiplies the positive class's loss. Focal loss down-weights already-easy examples so training concentrates on hard ones. Dice optimises overlap directly but is insensitive to small fires — which are the hard and operationally important cases — so CLAUDE.md rules it out as a sole objective.

Metrics & baselines

Accuracy is useless at this imbalance. These are the measures that mean something.

TP · FP · FN
Per pixel: predicted burn that did burn; predicted burn that did not; actual burn that was missed. True negatives are deliberately excluded from every metric below — there are millions of them and they are trivially correct.
CSI · IoU critical success index / intersection over union
TP / (TP + FP + FN). The same quantity under two names — CSI in meteorology, IoU in computer vision. The headline score, and the checkpoint selection criterion.
POD · FAR
Probability of detection, TP/(TP+FN), is the fraction of real spread caught. False alarm ratio, FP/(TP+FP), is the fraction of predicted spread that was wrong. They trade off against each other, which is why CSI is reported alongside both.
Brier score
Mean squared error of the probabilities themselves. Measures calibration — whether "70% likely" is right about 70% of the time — which threshold-based metrics cannot see.
Persistence baseline
Predict that nothing changes: tomorrow's burned area equals today's. Deceptively strong, because most of a fire's area on any given day was already burning. Any model that cannot beat it has learned nothing.
Circular spread baseline
Expand the perimeter uniformly outward at the training-set median rate. Tests whether the model has learned anything directional — wind, slope, fuel — beyond mere growth.

Training mechanics

Mostly hardware-shaped. This runs on an 8 GB laptop GPU, which is a real constraint rather than a footnote.

VRAM · batch size · gradient accumulation
GPU memory holds the model, the activations and the gradients; batch size is how many samples are processed together. Gradient accumulation sums gradients over several small batches before stepping, giving a large batch's statistics at a small batch's memory cost.
in this projectMeasured 0.602 GB per sample at 256 px: batch 11 fits in 8 GB, not the 16 CLAUDE.md specifies.
AMP automatic mixed precision
Running most operations in 16-bit while keeping a 32-bit master copy of the weights. Roughly halves activation memory and speeds up matrix work at negligible accuracy cost.
Gradient clipping
Capping gradient magnitude before the optimiser step. Recurrent networks are prone to exploding gradients as updates compound through time; clipping at 1.0 is the standard guard.
Cosine annealing with warm restarts
A learning-rate schedule that decays along a cosine curve then jumps back up. The restarts help escape sharp minima and often improve generalisation over monotonic decay.
Overfit test
Deliberately training on a single fire until the model memorises it. Not a result — a wiring check. If a network cannot overfit one example, something upstream is broken, and this is the cheapest place to discover that.
Early stopping
Halting when validation stops improving for a set number of epochs, and keeping the best checkpoint — selected here on validation CSI rather than loss, since loss is dominated by the easy negatives.

Terms coined here

Not standard vocabulary — names given to decisions made in this codebase, recorded so the code reads consistently.

Fire event segmentation
FIRMS supplies unlabelled points, not fires. Segmentation groups them into discrete events so the data can be split and reasoned about per fire.
in this project741,236 detections resolved into 692 usable fires.
Single-link clustering · union-find
Two detections join the same fire if within 2 km and 96 hours of each other, and the relation is then made transitive. Union-find is the data structure computing that closure efficiently. Single-link is the right choice because a fire creeps: its ends may be far apart while every step between them is close.
Link distance · link time
The two clustering thresholds. Too loose and separate fires merge into a blob; too tight and one fire shatters into fragments across overpass gaps.
burn_new vs cumulative
Storing each window's newly detected burn rather than the running total. The cumulative label is then an OR up to T and the target an OR over the horizon — one representation serving any horizon, without rewriting the same pixels into every later timestep.
usable · gap_flag
Per-window flags marking whether it may serve as a prediction target. An unobserved window is not evidence a fire stopped, so training on it teaches something false. Cumulative labels remain valid across such windows; only targets are restricted.
Dilation vs closing
Two operations CLAUDE.md merges into one. Dilation grows each detection to approximate the 375 m VIIRS footprint. Closing — dilate then erode — bridges gaps between detections without pushing the outer boundary outward.
in this projectDilation alone left the Camp mask in 274 disconnected pieces. With closing at radius 4, Creek, Caldor and Mosquito all land within 7% of official acreage.
Materialise vs crop on the fly
Whether to write every training tile to disk in advance, or store per-fire rasters and cut tiles at load time.
in this projectMaterialising costs 254 GB against 83 GB free. Cropping on the fly costs ~11 GB — and makes overlapping tiles free rather than duplicated.
Extract-and-discard
Stream a large remote file, keep only the small region needed, delete the original. Retaining raw HRRR for all 692 fires would cost ~204 GB; the extracted windows are 4.52 GB.
